# Can foreigners buy a house?

Yes. According to WorkinNL, the Dutch government's information desk for international workers, you can buy a house in the Netherlands as a foreigner, even if you are not from the European Union. What matters is a regular income, savings of your own, or both. If you come from outside the EU you need a valid residence permit, and sometimes stricter rules apply and you must show extra documents.

Buying a home does not give you a right to live here. Whether you get a mortgage is up to the lender, who looks at your income, your contract and your residence status.

# From viewing to notary

1. **Borrowing room.** Find out first how much you can borrow. Our [mortgage calculator](/en/mortgage-calculator/) gives a first indication.
2. **Search and view.** Compare prices by city, such as [Amsterdam](/en/house-prices/amsterdam/), [Utrecht](/en/house-prices/utrecht/) or [The Hague](/en/house-prices/den-haag/), or browse the [map](/en/map/).
3. **Make an offer.** Offers often include conditions (voorbehouden), for example on financing. How often buyers pay above asking is covered in [overbidding in the Netherlands](overbidding-netherlands).
4. **Purchase contract and cooling-off.** After signing, a private buyer has three days to change their mind without giving a reason.
5. **Mortgage and valuation.** You finalise the mortgage and have the home valued.
6. **Notary.** At the notary you sign the transfer deed and the mortgage deed, and you get the keys.

The whole process is in [house-buying steps in the Netherlands](house-buying-steps-netherlands).

# BSN, contract and residency

Banks and notaries work with your BSN (citizen service number), your ID and proof of income such as an employer's statement and payslips. The exact documents differ by lender. On a fixed-term contract? Read [mortgage on a temporary contract](mortgage-temporary-contract). On the 30% ruling? Read [the 30% ruling and your mortgage](30-percent-ruling-mortgage).

# Costs

On top of the price you pay the [buying costs (kosten koper)](buying-costs-netherlands):

- transfer tax: **{{ttOwn}}** if you will live in the home, **{{ttNotMain}}** if it will not be your main residence;
- the first-time buyer exemption (**{{ttStarter}}**) if you are {{starterAgeMin}} or older and younger than {{starterAgeMax}}, will live in the home, have not used the exemption before and the price is not above {{starterMax}}. The conditions on Rijksoverheid.nl do not mention nationality;
- notary, valuation and advice (market prices; get quotes);
- with NHG, a one-off {{nhgFeePct}} of the mortgage amount.

Your mortgage may not exceed {{ltvMax}} of the market value, so plan to pay the extra costs from savings.

# Timeline

There is no legal timeline. The only fixed period is the three-day cooling-off after you sign the purchase contract. How long searching, negotiating and financing take depends on the market and your situation. The transfer date is agreed in the purchase contract.

# Worked example

A 38-year-old expat buys a €400,000 home to live in. Because of their age, the first-time buyer exemption does not apply.

- Transfer tax at {{ttOwn}}: **€8,000**.
- Had they been 34 and met the other conditions, it would be **{{ttStarter}}**, since €400,000 is below {{starterMax}}.
- Add notary, valuation and advice; enter those amounts yourself in the [buying costs calculator](/en/buying-costs-calculator/).
