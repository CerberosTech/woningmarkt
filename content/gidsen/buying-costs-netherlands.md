# What does kosten koper mean?

"Kosten koper" (k.k.) means that you, the buyer, pay the extra costs of the transfer. Dutch asking prices are almost always "k.k.". On top of the price you pay:

- transfer tax (sometimes none, see below);
- the notary for the transfer deed and the mortgage deed;
- a valuation of the home;
- mortgage advice or arrangement;
- with an NHG mortgage: the fee for the National Mortgage Guarantee;
- sometimes a bank guarantee or a building survey.

# Transfer tax and the first-time buyer exemption

Transfer tax (overdrachtsbelasting) in {{year}}:

- **{{ttOwn}}** of the price if you will live in the home (main residence);
- **{{ttStarter}}** with the first-time buyer exemption;
- **{{ttNotMain}}** for a home that will not be your main residence, such as a home to let;
- **{{ttOther}}** for other property.

You get the first-time buyer exemption only if you meet every condition:

1. You are {{starterAgeMin}} or older and younger than {{starterAgeMax}}.
2. You have not used the exemption before.
3. You will live in the home yourself.
4. The price is not above {{starterMax}}.

If the price is above {{starterMax}}, you pay {{ttOwn}} on the whole amount, not only on the part above the limit.

# Notary, valuation and advice

These are market prices. No law sets an amount, and they differ by provider and by home. Ask for quotes and compare them. In the [buying costs calculator](/en/buying-costs-calculator/) you enter the amounts from your quotes yourself. We do not fill in an amount for you.

With an NHG mortgage you pay a one-off fee of {{nhgFeePct}} of the mortgage amount. NHG is possible if the loan is not above {{nhgLimit}}, or {{nhgLimitEBV}} with energy-saving measures.

# Can you include the buying costs in the mortgage?

Usually not. Your mortgage may not exceed {{ltvMax}} of the market value of the home. So you normally pay the buying costs from your own savings. To see how much you can borrow on your income, use the [mortgage calculator](/en/mortgage-calculator/).
