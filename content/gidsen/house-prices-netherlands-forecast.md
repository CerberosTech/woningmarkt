# The trend according to CBS and the Kadaster

The price index for existing owner-occupied homes, published by CBS (Statistics Netherlands) and the Kadaster (land registry), is the official measure of price change. The latest monthly figure (published 22 September 2026):

- in August 2026 existing homes were on average **3.3% more expensive** than a year earlier, down from 3.9% in July;
- compared with July, prices fell 0.1%;
- according to CBS, annual price growth has been slowing almost continuously since late 2024;
- prices were 17.8% above the previous peak of July 2022.

The average sale price over the whole of {{dataYear}} was {{avgPriceNL}} (CBS, updated {{cbsUpdated}}). Note that an average sale price is not a price index: it also shifts when a different mix of homes is sold. Yearly figures and city detail are on [house prices](/en/house-prices/).

# What DNB and the banks expect

We do not make price forecasts ourselves. This is what others expect, with publication dates:

- **DNB (Dutch central bank), Spring Projections (12 June 2026):** existing home prices rising **3 to 4% a year** from 2026 to 2028, clearly lower than a year earlier, because of weaker consumer confidence and higher mortgage rates. More landlords selling rental homes could make the rise slightly lower. (In its December 2025 Autumn Projections DNB still expected about 4% for both 2026 and 2027.)
- **Rabobank, Housing Market Quarterly (9 September 2026):** **4.2%** in 2026 and **3.2%** in 2027, revised up from its previous quarterly (2.8% and 2.0%).
- **ABN AMRO, Housing Market Monitor (1 October 2026):** **3.8%** in 2026 and **2.5%** in 2027. ABN AMRO expects a sharper slowdown in 2027 as mortgage rates stay higher for longer, but "for now" no fall in prices.

So all three expect slower growth in 2027 than in recent years, in a range of 2.5% to 4%. Forecasts are revised regularly.

# Regional differences

The national average hides big gaps:

- **Rabobank (9 September 2026)** expects about 2% growth in 2026 in Greater Amsterdam and the Zaanstreek, against about 8% in East Groningen and Southeast Drenthe. It expects the gaps to narrow next year.
- **ABN AMRO (1 October 2026)** cites second-quarter growth of 0.8% in Amsterdam against 4.2% nationally and 7.9% in the province of Groningen.
- **NVM (Q3 2026)** reports 0.6% annual growth in Greater Amsterdam, against 8.3% in Southwest Drenthe and a slight fall in areas including the Haarlem and Leiden agglomerations. This covers homes sold through NVM agents.

See CBS prices by municipality on the [map](/en/map/) or by city, for example [Amsterdam](/en/house-prices/amsterdam/), [Groningen](/en/house-prices/groningen/), [Utrecht](/en/house-prices/utrecht/) and [Rotterdam](/en/house-prices/rotterdam/).

# Worked example

As an illustration, not a prediction: what would the 2027 forecasts mean for a home worth €450,000 at the end of 2026?

- At 2.5% (ABN AMRO): about **€461,250**.
- At 3.2% (Rabobank): about **€464,400**.
- At 3% to 4% (DNB): about **€463,500 to €468,000**.

These are national averages. A single home or neighbourhood can do very differently, including falling.
