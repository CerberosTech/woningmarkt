# Income and the housing-cost ratio

In the Netherlands your bank does not decide freely how much you may borrow. The government sets the limits in the Temporary Mortgage Credit Regulation (Tijdelijke regeling hypothecair krediet, Trhk). It contains a table of **financing-cost percentages** (financieringslastpercentages, often called the woonquote): the share of your gross income that may go to interest and repayment. Nibud, the national budget institute, advises on the table every year and the ministry sets it.

The percentage depends on your test income and the interest rate used in the test. A higher income gives a higher percentage. The result is a maximum monthly payment, which is converted into a loan amount using the rate and a 30-year term.

There is also a cap based on the home: the mortgage may not exceed {{ltvMax}} of the market value. That is why you normally pay the [buying costs](buying-costs-netherlands) from savings.

# The test rate

If you fix your rate for less than 10 years, the lender must test with at least {{toetsrenteMin}}, even if your actual rate is lower. The AFM, the Dutch financial markets authority, sets this minimum each quarter. Fix for 10 years or longer and your actual rate is used. A longer fixed period can therefore give you more borrowing room, even at a slightly higher rate. See [mortgage rate outlook](mortgage-rate-forecast-netherlands) for how to think about fixed periods.

# Student debt and partners

**Student debt.** Since 2024 lenders use the actual monthly amount you pay to DUO, the student finance agency, not an estimate based on the total debt. That amount is grossed up with a factor from the Trhk (it depends on the test rate) and subtracted from your maximum monthly payment. If you have not started repaying yet, have a repayment holiday or pay a reduced amount, the lender works with your current balance, rate and remaining term.

**Partner.** According to Nibud, the lower earner's income has counted in full since 2023. Two people with the same combined gross income can borrow the same amount, however the income is split.

**Energy label.** A home with a good energy label, or a plan for energy-saving measures, adds a fixed amount of borrowing room. The amounts are in the Trhk and built into our calculator.

# The NHG limit

If your mortgage stays at or below {{nhgLimit}} ({{nhgLimitEBV}} with energy-saving measures), you can take out a mortgage with the National Mortgage Guarantee in {{year}}. NHG does not change how much you may borrow, but it affects your rate and your safety net. See [NHG mortgage explained](nhg-mortgage-explained).

# Worked example

A single buyer earning €60,000 gross a year, no student debt, a home with energy label E, and a rate of 4.0% fixed for 10 years. Our [mortgage calculator](/en/mortgage-calculator/) uses the 2026 Trhk tables:

- At this income and rate the Trhk table allows a gross monthly payment of about €1,130.
- As a 30-year annuity mortgage: about **€236,700**. Label E adds no extra room.
- Same case with a 5-year fixed rate: the {{toetsrenteMin}} test rate applies and the result is about **€229,100**.
- Paying €100 a month to DUO (10-year fixed): the amount falls to about **€211,600**.

These are indications from our own calculator. A lender also looks at other debts, your contract and your age, and may arrive at a different figure.
